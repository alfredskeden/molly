1177 vaccination page (sv, updated 2025-11-18): https://www.1177.se/undersokning-behandling/vaccinationer/vaccinationsprogrammet-for-barn/
- Same schedule as above; vaccination is voluntary and free.
- Rotavirus: the baby shouldn't eat just before the drops, because they may vomit them up. Short-lived loose stools, vomiting or fussiness are common. Very rarely the bowel folds into itself (intussusception); go to an emergency department at once if you suspect it.
- After the 3, 5 and 12 m shots, a baby may be tired, eat less or get a fever a couple of days later.
- After the 18 m MMR shot, a fever and rash can come 1–2 weeks later. The rash is not contagious.

1177 English version: https://www.1177.se/en/undersokning-behandling/vaccinationer/vaccinationsprogrammet-for-barn/ (200, English)

### 1c. International check-ups and vaccines [INT]

  - Physical exam within 72 h of birth (eyes, heart, hips, testicles).
  - New baby review at 10–14 days by a health visitor (safe sleep, feeding, vaccines, adjusting to being a parent).

  - The AAP recommends general developmental screening with validated tools at 9, 18 and 30 m, and autism screening at 18 and 24 m (or whenever there's a concern).

## 2. Health facts by age band

### Fever: the rules that apply across ages

**[SE] 1177, Swedish** (updated 2023-06-29): https://www.1177.se/sjukdomar--besvar/infektioner/feber/feber-hos-barn/
- Fever usually means 38.0 °C or higher. Normal is about 36–37.8 °C. How the child seems matters more than the number.
- Measure in the bottom if the child is under 1 year (ear or bottom are most reliable).
- **Contact a vårdcentral or out-of-hours clinic straight away if:**
  - the temperature is 41.0 °C or higher
  - **the baby is under 3 months** (any fever)
  - **the baby is under 6 months and has no cold symptoms at all**
  - the child seems very ill, has a lot of pain or cries inconsolably
  - the child refuses all drinks or shows signs of dehydration (dry mouth, peeing less)
  - the child has been in a tropical country and has a headache or body aches
- If you can't reach those, go to an emergency department (akutmottagning).
- Fever for more than 4 days: contact a vårdcentral, and wait for it to open if it's closed.
- First-time febrile seizure: call 112. Febrile seizures are uncommon and look frightening, but aren't harmful.
- Medicine:
  - Call 1177 or a vårdcentral before giving *any* medicine to a baby under 6 months.
  - Over-the-counter fever medicine only if the child is uncomfortable, won't drink, or can't settle.
  - Don't treat fever in a child who otherwise feels well.
  - No aspirin under 18.
- Give more to drink than usual.
- Back to preschool once the child is fever-free for 24 h without medicine and can manage a full day.

**[SE] 1177, English** (same revision date 2023-06-29): https://www.1177.se/en/sjukdomar--besvar/infektioner/feber/feber-hos-barn/
- **Differs from the Swedish page:** its urgent list says "younger than six months and has a fever", with no 3-month split and no cold-symptom condition. It also has nothing on febrile seizures, 112 or medicine.
- Recommendation: base the app on the Swedish page. "Under 3 months with any fever, or under 6 months with fever and no cold symptoms: seek care straight away" is the stricter, more detailed rule. Link the English page as the English reading.
- It gives the phone numbers 1177 and "0046 771 11 77 00" from foreign numbers. The nurse speaks Swedish and English, and sometimes other languages.

**[INT] NHS, "Fever in children"** (last reviewed 3 Jan 2024)
- Final URL: https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/ (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/ redirects there).
- https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/high-temperature-in-children/ is a **404**.
- A high temperature is 38C or more.
- **Call 111 urgently if:**
  - under 3 months with 38C or higher
  - 3–6 months with 39C or higher
  - the temperature lasts 5 days or more
  - there are other signs of illness, such as a rash, or the child is dehydrated
- **Call 999 if:**
  - stiff neck
  - a rash that doesn't fade under a pressed glass
  - a first febrile seizure
  - blue, grey or pale skin, lips or tongue
  - drowsy and hard to wake
  - difficulty breathing
- Medicine:
  - no paracetamol under 2 months
  - no ibuprofen under 3 months or under 5 kg, with chickenpox, or if the child is dehydrated
  - no aspirin under 16

**[INT] NHS, "When to get urgent medical help for babies and children under 5"** (reviewed 6 Aug 2026): https://www.nhs.uk/baby/health/when-to-get-urgent-medical-help-for-babies-and-children-under-5/
- **Call 999 or go to A&E for any of these:**
  - 38C or higher under 3 months, or 39C or higher at 3–6 months
  - a temperature below 36C, or feeling cold or shivery
  - breathing fast or with difficulty (grunting, tummy sucking in)
  - blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin
  - cold, clammy skin
  - a rash that doesn't fade when pressed
  - a weak, high-pitched or continuous cry
  - hard to wake or can't be woken
  - no pee in 12 h
  - a seizure
  - swollen lips, tongue or throat
- Also listed as serious (no service named):
  - 38C or higher for more than 5 days (3 m–5 y)
  - floppy
  - a baby who isn't feeding
  - no drink for 8 h while awake, or vomiting for 24 h
  - a bulging soft spot on the head
- Call 111 if you're worried about any symptom.
- Note: this page tells you to **call 999 / A&E** for the same temperatures for which the fever page says "call 111 urgently". Use the stronger wording, or link both.

### Teeth and teething

- **[SE] 1177, teeth development** (sv, updated 2026-01-14): https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/sa-vaxer-och-utvecklas-barn/kroppen/tandernas-utveckling/
  - First teeth usually come at 6–8 m, normally the two lower front teeth and then the two upper. Being born with a tooth is rare.
  - First molars at about 1–1.5 y. There are 20 milk teeth (10 top, 10 bottom), and most children have them all by 3.
  - Teething can make the gums sore and itchy and cause more dribbling. A **slight** fever is possible, but talk to BVC if the child seems to be in pain, since the fever can have other causes.
  - Rub the gums gently with a finger, a soft toothbrush or a teething ring.
  - **Tell BVC if there are no teeth by about 1.5 y.**
  - `/en/` is a 404.
- **[SE] Rikshandboken, teeth 0–6 y** (approved 2024-07-31): https://www.rikshandboken-bhv.se/halsobesok/halsoundersokning/barnets-tander-0-6-ar/
  - First tooth at about 6 m, but it can take another half year or more.
  - All milk teeth through by 2.5–3 y. Boys and girls follow the same timing.
  - Teething can cause itching, dribbling, red swollen gums and occasionally a mild fever.
  - Teeth present at birth: contact dental care.
  - The page gives no paste amount.
- **[SE] 1177, tooth brushing** (sv, updated 2022-02-25): https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/praktiska-rad-i-vardagen/borsta-tander-pa-barn/
  - Start at the first tooth, twice a day (after breakfast and before bed).
  - Fluoride toothpaste, **no more than the size of the child's own little-finger nail** for the first teeth. No ppm given.
  - It's easiest to brush a small child lying down (changing table, or head in your lap).
  - Electric brush from about 3–4 y.
  - An adult helps until about 10–12 y.
  - Preferably don't rinse with water afterwards.
  - Under 2: let the child get used to the brush, and biting on it can soothe itchy gums.
  - `/en/` is a 404.
- **[INT] NHS teething** (reviewed 14 May 2026): https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/teething/baby-teething-symptoms/
  - Most babies start at around 6 m; some start before 4 m, some after 12 m, and some are born with a tooth.
  - Usual order:
    - front teeth around 6 m
    - the teeth beside them around 8 m
    - first molars around 12 m
    - canines around 18 m
    - second molars around 24 m
  - All milk teeth by 2–3 y. Bottom teeth often come before top teeth.
  - Symptoms: sore red gum, a slightly raised temperature **under 38C**, a flushed cheek, rubbing an ear, dribbling, gnawing, being fretful and sleeping less well.
  - There's no evidence that teething causes diarrhoea.
  - Tips page: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/teething/tips-for-helping-your-teething-baby/
- **[INT] NHS, looking after your baby's teeth** (reviewed 1 Apr 2025): https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/teething/looking-after-your-babys-teeth/
  - Brush as soon as teeth come through, at least twice a day, including just before bed.
  - Toothpaste: **under 3, a tiny smear**; **3–6 y, a pea-sized amount** (no ppm on the page).
  - Spit, don't rinse. Help until at least age 7.
  - Children's NHS dental treatment is free, but not every dentist takes new NHS patients.
  - No juice or sugary drinks in bottles. No honey under 1.

### Vitamin D

- **[SE] 1177, food up to 1 year** (updated 2025-09-16): https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/mat-och-dryck-for-barn/barnets-mat-upp-till-ett-ar/

## 3. Parents' corner

### Baby blues vs depression

- **[SE] 1177, depression around having a baby** (sv only; `/en/` is a 404; updated 2024-08-06): https://www.1177.se/sjukdomar--besvar/psykiska-sjukdomar-och-besvar/depression/depression-i-samband-med-att-du-far-barn/
  - Baby blues: low mood, mood swings and tiredness starting **2–3 days after the birth** and usually **passing within 2 weeks**. It doesn't mean you have depression.
  - Depression: feeling low **almost every day for more than 2 weeks**. Other signs:
    - losing joy in things
    - sleep problems and exhaustion
    - guilt and hopelessness
    - anxiety and appetite changes
    - finding it hard to care for the baby
    - thoughts of harming yourself or the baby
  - Depression usually eases within about half a year with help.
  - **It can also affect a co-parent or adoptive parent.**
  - EPDS 6–8 weeks after the birth, followed by a talk with the BVC nurse. A non-birth parent can have an individual talk at BVC or fill in a rating form at a vårdcentral.
  - Help: vårdcentral, barnmorskemottagning (midwife clinic) or BVC; call 1177 for advice.
  - Postpartum psychosis (2 weeks to 3 months after birth): psychiatric emergency or general emergency department, or **112**.
  - Treatment: talk therapy, and medicines that are safe while breastfeeding.
  - No percentages are given.
- **[SE] 1177, how you may feel after the birth** (sv only; `/en/` is a 404; updated 2025-03-13): https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/forlossning/efter-forlossningen/sa-kan-du-ma-efter-forlossningen/
  - Worry and low mood are common after a birth; mood can swing between happy and low. Many feel like a bad parent, or ashamed for not feeling happy.
  - For many it gets better after **1–2 weeks**.
  - Rest; sleep when the baby sleeps; let others help, and help each other if you're two.
  - If you feel very unwell or don't recognise yourself: vårdcentral, barnmorskemottagning or BVC. 1177 for advice.
  - A follow-up visit at the midwife clinic is offered some time after the birth (mainly a physical check).
  - The page doesn't use the term "baby blues".
- **[INT] NHS postnatal depression** (reviewed 18 Mar 2026; final URL as given, 200): https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/postnatal-depression/
  - Baby blues usually goes within 2 weeks of the birth. PND can start in pregnancy, soon after the birth, or **up to a year after**. It often improves with treatment within 3–6 months.
  - **Fathers and partners can also get depression after a baby.**
  - Signs:
    - low mood, not enjoying things
    - hopelessness, guilt
    - anxiety, irritability
    - sleep problems, poor concentration
    - trouble bonding
    - thoughts of self-harm or of harming the baby
  - Help: GP, midwife or health visitor, "even if you only have some of the signs". You can self-refer to NHS talking therapies (18+, 16+ in some areas). Perinatal mental health teams. Samaritans 116 123.
  - Urgent help: https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/mental-health-services/where-to-get-urgent-help-for-mental-health/
  - The page gives no prevalence figure.
- **[INT] NHS postpartum psychosis** (reviewed 18 Oct 2023): https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-partum-psychosis/
  - About 1 in 1,000 mothers. It usually starts suddenly within the first 2 weeks.
  - Signs: hallucinations, delusions, mania, confusion, rapidly changing moods.
  - It is a medical emergency, and the person may not realise they're ill.
  - What to do: see a GP the same day, call 111, or call 999 / go to A&E if anyone may be in danger.
- **[INT] WHO perinatal mental health** (no page date): https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/promotion-prevention/perinatal-mental-health
  - The old `.../maternal-mental-health` URL redirects here.
  - Worldwide, about **13% of women who have just given birth** (10% in pregnancy) have a mental disorder, mainly depression. The figure is higher in low- and middle-income countries (19.8% after birth).
  - Fathers are not mentioned.
- **Prevalence:** I found no "1 in 10" figure on the NHS or 1177 pages. Use the WHO 13% (credited) or leave the number out. No source checked gave a figure for partners.

### EPDS at BVC [SE]

Rikshandboken (approved 2024-04-30): https://www.rikshandboken-bhv.se/foraldraskapsstod/individuellt-foraldraskapsstod/screening-med-epds/
- 10 questions about the last 7 days; a score of 12 or more prompts a closer look. It is not a diagnosis.
- Offered to everyone who gave birth, at a talk when the baby is 6–8 weeks.
- For the non-birth parent it is used when there's a reason, e.g. signs at the 3–5 m talk. It is validated in Sweden with the same cut-off, but general screening of fathers is advised against.
- If the birth parent is unwell, the other parent's wellbeing should be checked too.
- Some regions repeat EPDS at 4–6 and 8–12 m.
- Follow-up: support talks with the BVC nurse, or referral to a BVC psychologist, primary care or psychiatry.
- Only "3–4% of all new mothers" still have it through the first year (about 30% of those with PPD). This is a persistence figure, not prevalence.

### Parent's sleep and tiredness, asking for help

- **[INT] NHS, sleep and tiredness after having a baby** (reviewed 24 Aug 2026): https://www.nhs.uk/baby/support-and-services/sleep-and-tiredness-after-having-a-baby/
  - Rest when the baby sleeps; rest matters more than housework.
  - Share the nights: a partner can do formula feeds, nappies, or an occasional bottle of expressed milk.
  - Ask friends and family to watch the baby while you nap. A short daily walk helps. It's temporary.
  - Signs it may be PND: can't sleep even when the baby sleeps, constantly tired, feeling hopeless, losing interest. Speak to a GP or health visitor.
  - UK helplines on the page: Cry-sis 0800 448 0737 (9am–10pm), Coram Family Lives 0808 800 2222, Home-Start. These are UK-only and probably not for the app.
- **[SE] 1177, struggling as a parent** (sv; no updated date found): https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/nar-familjelivet-ar-svart/att-ma-daligt-som-foralder/
  - This is a hub page about separation, illness in the family and parents with mental illness.
  - On separation: try to cooperate about the children.
  - It mentions no BVC, helplines or 112. **Weak source for "asking for help"**; prefer the after-birth and depression pages above.
- **Relationship / co-parenting:**
  - [SE] BVC's 3–5 m talk with the non-birth parent covers how they are adjusting and how the relationship is going (1177 BVC page).
  - [SE] Parent groups in the first year for all guardians (Rikshandboken PDF).
  - [INT] NHS 2–2.5 y review encourages both parents to attend; partners can share night feeds (NHS sleep page).

### Where to get help

**Sweden:**
- **BVC:** the parents' talks and EPDS.
- **Barnmorskemottagning** (midwife clinic): includes the postnatal follow-up.
- **Vårdcentral** (health centre).
- **1177:** advice by phone around the clock. From a foreign phone: 0046 771-11 77 00 / +46 771-11 77 00. The 1177 pages write 0046.
- **112:** emergencies, postpartum psychosis, a first febrile seizure.

1177 "När du ringer 1177" (sv): https://www.1177.se/om-1177/nar-du-ringer-1177/ (`/en/` is a 404)
- Confirms 1177 is the national number for health advice, open around the clock.
- **It does not list the foreign-phone number.** That number is on the English BVC and English fever pages (written "0046 771-11 77 00" / "0046 771 11 77 00").
- Languages: the English fever page says the nurse speaks Swedish and English, sometimes other languages.

**UK / international:**
- GP, midwife, health visitor.
- NHS 111.
- 999 / A&E in an emergency.
- Self-referral to NHS talking therapies.
- Samaritans 116 123.

Other countries: "your local health service / emergency number".
