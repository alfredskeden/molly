# SLEEP

## Sleep totals per 24 h by page

Sources:
- **S1 = 1177**, https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/barns-somn/barns-somn-i-olika-aldrar/ : 0–3 m **14–18 h**; 4–11 m **12–16 h** (of which 3–6 h by day, 9–10 h at night); 1–2 y **10–16 h** (of which about 2 h by day); 3–5 y **10–14 h**.
- **A = AASM consensus** (Paruthi S et al., "Recommended Amount of Sleep for Pediatric Populations: A Consensus Statement of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine", *J Clin Sleep Med* 2016), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4877308/ : 4–12 months **12–16 h incl. naps**; 1–2 years **11–14 h incl. naps**; 3–5 years **10–13 h incl. naps**. It gives **no recommendation under 4 months** because of wide normal variation and too little evidence.
- **N = NSF**, https://www.thensf.org/how-many-hours-of-sleep-do-you-really-need/ : 0–3 m **14–17 h** (includes daytime naps); 4–11 m **12–15 h**; 1–2 y **11–14 h** (worded "each night", and the page doesn't say whether naps count); 3–5 y **10–13 h**. It also says an extra hour or two either side "may be appropriate", depending on the child.
- **NHS**, https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/helping-your-baby-to-sleep/ (a looser alternative): newborn total "can be around 18 hours"; 3–6 m some sleep 5–8 h at night or longer; 6–12 m some sleep around 15 h, mostly at night; 12 m on around 12–15 h; 2 y about 12–14 h including naps; 3–5 y about 12 h, and some still nap.

## Nap counts (only what a source actually says)

- **1177 is the only checked source with any nap number:** "Many children go from two naps to one at about 1.5–2 years." It also gives daytime sleep amounts: 4–11 m 3–6 h by day; 1–2 y about 2 h by day. On 3–5 y it says most children have stopped napping by 5; if a child still seems to need a nap, it suggests a 25–45 minute rest away from bedtime. 1177 gives **no nap count for 0–12 m**. (S1)
- **NHS:** no nap number or length at any age, only that some 3–5 year olds still need a daytime nap. (NHS sleep page)
- **AASM and NSF:** totals only, **no nap counts**.
- **AAP:** nap counts weren't checked (only the safe-sleep and solids pages were in scope).
- **So no International source gives nap counts by age.** The International pages should give none, or credit 1177's two-to-one transition as the only sourced statement.

## Night waking and night feeds

Sweden (S1, 1177 sleep page):
- 0–3 m: waking several times a night is very common.
- After about 2–4 m, babies usually sleep longer stretches at night.
- At 1 y, a "full night" usually means about 5 hours in a row.
- Some children sleep through within a few months and settle themselves; others still need help to fall back asleep when they are older.
- Most babies up to about 4 m still feed at night (for hunger, closeness, warmth and comfort). From about 4–6 m they usually don't need night feeds if they gain weight well. Tips for stopping: offer water instead, help the child eat more during the day, have another adult take the first nights, and comfort in other ways (nappy change, carrying, rocking).
- 1177 breastfeeding page (SV, https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-skota-ett-nyfott-barn/amning-och-flaskmatning/amning/): most babies wake several times a night to feed, newborns need night feeds, and how often varies a lot.

International (NHS sleep page):
- Newborns wake repeatedly at night for the first few months.
- 3–6 m: fewer night feeds.
- 6–12 m: some no longer need night feeds; teething or hunger may wake some.
- No age is given for sleeping through: some babies do it soon, others take a long time.
- Bedtime routine: a simple, soothing routine can start from the start, with no age given.

## Safe sleep / SIDS

**Sweden (1177 SV page https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-skota-ett-nyfott-barn/plotslig-spadbarnsdod--forebyggande-rad/ ; EN https://www.1177.se/en/barn--gravid/att-skota-ett-nyfott-barn/plotslig-spadbarnsdod--forebyggande-rad/):**
- Put the baby on the back to sleep; this is the most important advice. Side sleeping is less safe (EN: the baby could roll onto the tummy). The risk falls as the baby gets older and can turn over alone. No age is given for when to stop.
- Under 3 months, the safest place is the baby's own bed in the parents' room. Feeding or cuddling in the parents' bed is fine, but put the baby back in their own bed, on the back, before anyone sleeps.
- Sharing a bed with a baby under 3 months is linked to higher risk (bedding or an arm over the face, or the baby rolling).
- Keep blankets, pillows and toys away from the face. Avoid a very soft mattress (SV). The baby should be "moderately warm, not too warm" and dressed for the room temperature. **No temperature figure is given.**
- A dummy is fine for sleep; sucking may lower the risk, though why is unclear (SV only).
- No smoking or nicotine in pregnancy or after birth, including second-hand smoke; breastfeeding mothers should also stop snus. If stopping fully isn't possible, cutting down still helps.
- Alcohol and sedatives raise the risk of covering or lying on the baby.
- Breastfeeding lowers the risk.
- SIDS is very rare: about 1 child in 6,000 in Sweden. It is most common before 6 months (SV).
- Supervised tummy time while awake; vary the head position (SV).
- Swaddling: not mentioned.

**International (NHS https://www.nhs.uk/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/sudden-infant-death-syndrome-sids/):**
- SIDS can happen up to 12 months, but is more likely in the first 6 months. For babies born early, the advice applies for a year from the due date.
- Back to sleep, feet-to-foot, until the baby can roll both ways alone.
- Use a separate cot or Moses basket in the same room as an adult for every sleep, for at least the first 6 months. Firm, flat mattress.
- **Room 16–20 °C.** Take off a layer if the baby is hot or sweaty, take hats off indoors, and don't use hot water bottles or electric blankets or put the baby near a heater or in direct sun.
- No pillows, duvets, bumpers, loose blankets, soft toys, pods or nests in the cot; a baby sleeping bag is fine.
- Don't bed-share if the baby was born before 37 weeks or under 2.5 kg, or if an adult smokes, has had 2 or more units of alcohol, or has taken drugs or drowsy medicine. Never sleep on a sofa or armchair with the baby.
- Consider a dummy for settling to sleep.
- Don't swaddle a bed-sharing baby. Slings: T.I.C.K.S., no feeding in a sling, and take the baby out of a car seat on arrival.
- Breastfeed if you can, and keep vaccines up to date.

**International (AAP, links only: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx):** back to sleep for every sleep up to 1 year; a firm, flat surface (an incline over 10° is unsafe); share a room, not a bed, for at least 6 months; no bed-sharing "under any circumstances"; no soft objects; avoid weighted sleep products; one layer more than an adult and no hat indoors; a dummy at sleep, once breastfeeding is established for breastfed babies; stop swaddling when rolling starts (typically around 3–4 months); move a baby who falls asleep in a car seat, swing or carrier onto a flat surface; home monitors don't prevent SIDS. AAP is all rights reserved, so link only.

**Difference to note:** NHS and AAP say to share a room for at least 6 months, while 1177 says own bed in the parents' room under 3 months. AAP forbids bed-sharing; NHS and 1177 describe the risks and when not to. Only NHS gives a room temperature (16–20 °C).

# FEEDING

## Milk feeding (0–6 m)

Sweden:
- Only breast milk or formula for the first 6 months. Breastfeeding can go on through the first year or longer. (1177 food 0–1 https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/mat-och-dryck-for-barn/barnets-mat-upp-till-ett-ar/ ; 1177 EN breastfeeding)
- The EN page says solids come after 6 months, with milk or formula continuing until at least 1 year. (1177 EN breastfeeding https://www.1177.se/en/barn--gravid/att-skota-ett-nyfott-barn/amning-och-flaskmatning/amning/)
- No fixed number of feeds: babies often feed around the clock in the first days, and a very sleepy newborn may need waking to feed. (1177 SV breastfeeding)
- Signs of enough milk: alert, wakes and wants to feed, content after feeds, weight gain checked at BVC, **at least 6 soaked nappies a day**, and stools going from black to yellow in the first week. (1177 EN and SV breastfeeding)
- Help: the maternity ward (BB) in the first week, then BVC, a midwife clinic or a breastfeeding clinic, plus 1177 at any hour (+46 771 11 77 00 from a foreign phone, English spoken). (1177 EN breastfeeding)
- Formula: infant formula for the first 6 months; follow-on formula is for babies over 6 m who eat solids and isn't meant as the only food. A baby kept on infant formula after 6 m may need a little extra iron. Use only formula approved in Sweden, made with cold tap water, dosed by the pack, served lukewarm, with no microwave, and made fresh for each meal (keep no more than 1 hour). No amounts or frequency are given. (1177 EN formula https://www.1177.se/en/barn--gravid/att-skota-ett-nyfott-barn/amning-och-flaskmatning/brostmjolksersattning-och-tillskottsnaring/)

International:
- WHO: start breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth; breast milk only for the first 6 months; responsive feeding day and night; continue breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond. (WHO fact sheet https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/infant-and-young-child-feeding)
- NHS: no fixed feed count, feed as often and as long as the baby wants. Nappies: 2–3 wet in the first 48 h; from day 4, at least 2 soft yellow poos a day (about the size of a £2 coin) for the first few weeks; **from day 5, at least 6 heavy wet nappies per 24 h**. Babies may lose some weight in the first 3–4 days, then gain steadily. Ask early for help from a midwife, health visitor or breastfeeding specialist. (https://www.nhs.uk/baby/breastfeeding-and-bottle-feeding/breastfeeding-problems/enough-milk/)

## Starting solids, by age

**Sweden**
- **From 4 m at the earliest: tiny tastes (smakprov)** if the baby is curious. About a "kryddmått" (a pinch) on a finger or spoon, small enough not to replace milk. Milk stays the full diet until 6 m. (Livsmedelsverket SV spädbarn https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/matvanor-halsa--miljo/kostrad/barn-och-ungdomar/spadbarn/ ; 1177 food 0–1 ; 1177 taste portions https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/mat-och-dryck-for-barn/laga-smakportioner-och-egen-barnmat/)
- **About 6 m: taste portions (smakportioner)**, starting with one or a few teaspoons (about 1 tsp in the first days) and growing slowly. Food should be soft; soft pieces the baby can pick up are fine too. Start with cooked potato, vegetables, meat, lentils, chicken, egg, fish or baby porridge; banana, apple or pear are fine. (1177 food 0–1 ; LV spädbarn ; 1177 taste portions)
- **About 8 m: small meals.** Wholegrain välling from about 8 m. (LV spädbarn ; 1177 food 0–1)
- **10–12 m:** two main meals a day plus some snacks (LV spädbarn). Lightly fried dishes can start (1177 taste portions).
- **About 1 y:** roughly the same food as the family, chopped or mashed as needed (1177 taste portions ; 1177 food 1–6 ; LV EN 1–2 y). By the end of the first year most babies eat ordinary food at every meal (1177 food 0–1).
- Texture: purée, mash or finely chopped food until the baby can chew, then coarser. For the first days of a new food you can mix in breast milk or formula. (1177 taste portions)
- Fat: under 2 y, add about 1 tsp oil per portion of home-made food, 2–3 times a day (1177 taste portions; 1177 food 1–6 says up to 1 tbsp a day in total). Over 2 y no extra fat is needed. (1177 food 1–6 https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/mat-och-dryck-for-barn/barnets-mat-1-6-ar/)
- **Iron:** babies under 1 need almost as much iron as an adult man. Iron-fortified Swedish baby porridge and välling help; give porridge or välling no more than 3 times a day. Other sources are meat, blood pudding, fish, beans, lentils and tofu, with fruit or vegetables (vitamin C) to help absorption. (LV spädbarn) Regular solids are needed from about 6 m for iron; dairy is low in iron, so larger amounts wait until after 1 y. (1177 food 0–1) Iron-fortified porridge or välling is useful in the 2nd year. (LV 1–2 y)
- **Allergens:** introduce gluten gradually from about 6 m, as tastes alongside breast milk or formula (LV spädbarn ; 1177 food 0–1). Introduce fish, egg and peanut (as butter or flour, never whole) during the first year, even if the family has allergies or eczema. Waiting past 1 y doesn't prevent allergy. Don't cut foods out on your own; ask BVC. (LV spädbarn) If a baby reacts to a food, contact a health centre (vårdcentral) or BVC. Redness round the mouth or on the bottom isn't always allergy. Egg and cow's-milk-protein allergies are the most common, and many children tolerate those foods by about 4 y. (1177 food 0–1 ; 1177 food 1–6)
- Ask BVC before giving any supplement other than vitamin D. (1177 food 0–1)

**International**
- WHO: complementary foods from 6 months, starting small and increasing with age; 2–3 meals a day at 6–8 m; 3–4 meals a day plus 1–2 snacks as needed at 9–23 m; gradually more texture and variety; responsive feeding. (WHO fact sheet)
- NHS: start **around 6 months**, when 3 signs show together (sits and holds the head steady; coordinates eyes, hands and mouth; swallows instead of pushing food out). Chewing fists, waking more at night or wanting more milk are *not* signs of readiness. Around 6 m: a few teaspoons or pieces before the milk feed. 7–9 m: work up to 3 meals plus about 4 milk feeds (formula-fed about 600 ml a day). 10–12 m: 3 meals plus about 3 milk feeds (formula about 400 ml a day). From 12 m: 3 meals plus 2 healthy snacks, and formula is no longer needed. Finger foods from the start. A new food may take 10 or more tries. Iron foods from about 7 m. Introduce peanut, egg, gluten and fish from around 6 m, one at a time in small amounts. (https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/babys-first-solid-foods/)
- AAP (link only): breast milk only for about 6 months, with breastfeeding continuing to 2 y or beyond; signs of readiness; one new single-ingredient food every 3–5 days; no cereal in the bottle; no evidence that delaying egg, dairy, soy, peanut or fish past 4–6 m prevents allergy (ask the doctor about peanut if severe eczema or egg allergy). (https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/feeding-nutrition/Pages/Starting-Solid-Foods.aspx)

## Drinks

**Sweden**
- Water is the best drink, with meals from about 6 m. Avoid sweet drinks, including juice. (1177 food 0–1)
- Cow's milk in cooking from 6 m. **Milk as a drink, and larger amounts of fil, yoghurt or plant drinks, wait until 1 y.** (1177 food 0–1 ; LV spädbarn)
- After 1 y: about **half a litre** of milk, fil, yoghurt or välling a day covers the calcium need. Choose **low-fat** milk for all ages, vitamin-fortified, with extra oil in food under 2 y. Fortified plant drinks are fine after 1 y. (1177 food 1–6 ; LV 1–2 y https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/matvanor-halsa--miljo/kostrad/barn-och-ungdomar/barn-1-2-ar/ ; LV 2–17 y https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/matvanor-halsa--miljo/kostrad/barn-och-ungdomar/barn-2-17-ar/)
- **No rice drinks under 6 y** (arsenic). (1177 ; LV)
- Run the tap until cold, because warm water can carry copper and cause diarrhoea. Test a private well (LV 1–2 y says every 3 years). (1177 food 1–6 ; LV 1–2 y)
- Juice, squash and soft drinks: avoid as long as possible (LV 1–2 y); juice counts as sweet even without added sugar (1177 food 1–6). Small children shouldn't have coffee, strong tea or cola, and no child should have energy drinks. No fennel or anise tea for children. (LV 1–2 y ; LV spädbarn)

**International**
- NHS: sips of water from an open or free-flow cup with meals from around 6 m. Cow's milk in cooking from 6 m; **whole cow's milk as the main drink from 12 months**. Unsweetened calcium-fortified plant drinks as the main drink from 1 y. 1% and skimmed milk as a main drink only from 5 y (fine in cooking from 1 y). **No rice drinks under 5.** (NHS first solids) No juice or smoothies for 4 years or younger. (NHS foods to avoid)
- AAP (link only): no juice under 12 months; at 12–36 months, at most 4 oz (about 120 ml) of 100% juice a day, in a cup. Small amounts of water with solids, up to 8 oz (about 240 ml) a day. (AAP starting solids)
- **Difference:** Sweden says low-fat milk at every age, while NHS says whole milk from 1 y and low-fat from 5 y. Rice drinks are banned under 6 y in Sweden and under 5 y in the UK.

## Vitamin D

- **Sweden:** D-drops every day for all babies, **from about 1 week to 2 years**, and they continue after solids start. Some children need them longer (rarely outdoors, covered skin in summer, no fish, no fortified foods). The drops can be paused May–August but can also be given all year; if unsure, give them. Breast milk has everything except vitamin D. **No dose is stated** on 1177 or LV. (1177 food 0–1 ; 1177 SV breastfeeding ; 1177 food 1–6 ; LV spädbarn ; LV 1–2 y ; LV 2–17 y)
- **UK (NHS):** breastfed babies get a daily **8.5–10 µg** vitamin D from birth to 1 y, even if the mother takes vitamin D. Babies on **more than 500 ml formula a day** get no supplement. Daily vitamins A, C and D for all children **6 months to 5 years**; 1–4 y take 10 µg vitamin D. Don't double up (e.g. cod liver oil plus drops). Free with Healthy Start. (https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/vitamins-for-children/ ; the first-solids page says the same for under 500 ml)
- **WHO:** the IYCF fact sheet makes **no vitamin D statement**.
- **AAP (link only):** ask the doctor about vitamin D and iron in the first year.

## Choking

- **Sweden prevention:** mash or cut food very small; offer soft pieces the baby can pick up; avoid whole nuts, almonds, grapes and peanuts for the youngest (1177 food 0–1 ; 1177 food 1–6). The child should sit still while eating, and not eat in a car or on a bike (1177 choking).
- **Sweden first aid** (https://www.1177.se/olyckor--skador/akuta-rad---forsta-hjalpen/nar-nagon-satter-i-halsen/, Swedish): under 1 y, alternate 5 back blows (face down along the forearm, head low) with 5 chest thrusts. Over 1 y, alternate 5 back blows with 5 abdominal thrusts. Call **112** if the child can't breathe, goes unconscious (then start CPR), a button battery is stuck, or you're unsure something is still there. A doctor should check the child afterwards even if the object came out.
- **International prevention:** stay with the baby while they eat. Gagging (watering eyes, tongue pushed forward, retching) is common and different from choking. (NHS first solids) AAP's choking hazards under 1 y (link only): hot dogs, nuts, seeds, chunks of meat or cheese, whole grapes, popcorn, chunks of peanut butter, raw vegetables, fruit chunks, hard or sticky sweets.
- **International first aid** (https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/): under 1 y, up to 5 back blows, then up to 5 chest thrusts (2 fingers below the nipple line). Over 1 y, up to 5 back blows, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts. Call 999 if it doesn't come out; get medical help afterwards anyway.

## First teeth and brushing (as they relate to food)

- **Sweden:** first teeth usually come at 6–8 m. Brush from the first tooth, twice a day (after breakfast and before bed), with fluoride toothpaste the size of the child's little-finger nail. Children need adult help until about 10–12 y. The page doesn't cover night bottles or sugar, apart from a teaser to avoid sweet snacks. (1177 brushing https://www.1177.se/barn--gravid/att-ta-hand-om-barn/praktiska-rad-i-vardagen/borsta-tander-pa-barn/)
- **UK:** start when the first tooth appears (usually around 6 m). Under 3 y, a parent brushes twice a day for about 2 minutes, last thing at night plus once more, with a **smear** of toothpaste with at least 1,000 ppm fluoride. From 3 y, a pea-sized amount (1,000–1,500 ppm); spit, don't rinse. See a dentist when the first teeth come or before 12 months. (https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/taking-care-of-childrens-teeth/) An open or free-flow cup without a valve is better for teeth (NHS first solids).

## When to seek help (as stated)

- **Sweden:** fewer than 6 soaked nappies a day is the opposite of the stated sign of enough milk (1177 breastfeeding); ask BB in the first week, then BVC. Diarrhoea or constipation after starting food: contact BVC or a paediatrician. A reaction to a food: vårdcentral or BVC. Picky eating is usually fine if the child is active and following the growth curve; otherwise ask BVC. (1177 food pages) Sleep problems: BVC; call 1177 for advice at any time. (1177 sleep)
- **International:** worried about milk intake: ask early (midwife, health visitor, breastfeeding specialist); signs are at least 6 heavy wet nappies from day 5 and at least 2 yellow poos from day 4 (NHS enough milk). Sleep problems: health visitor (NHS sleep). AAP (link): loose, watery or mucousy stools after starting solids mean cutting back and calling the doctor.
- No checked page states a hard "seek help if fewer than X nappies" rule beyond the expected counts above.
