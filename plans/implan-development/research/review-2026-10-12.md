# Development guide: adversarial content review

Scope: `packages/core/src/development/entries/*.ts`, `health.ts`, `sleep.ts`, `content.ts`, checked against `plans/implan-development/research/facts-*.md`, `MAIN.md` and `NOTES.md`. Only problems are listed, most severe first. Paths are relative to `packages/core/src/development/`.

Severity: **H** = safety (could falsely reassure about a red flag), **M** = wrong, overstated or alarming at that age, or a mixed message, **L** = wording, tone, age fit or close copying.

---

## High

### 1. (H) Emergency signs are listed like routine "ask BVC / your doctor" items
- **Where:**
  - `entries/health.ts`, International getHelp on every page range:
    - "Your baby is floppy, hard to wake, or has a weak, high-pitched or constant cry"
    - "Breathing fast or with difficulty, or skin, lips or tongue that look blue, grey, pale or blotchy"
    - "A rash that doesn't fade when you press a glass against it[, a stiff neck, or a seizure]"
  - `entries/sleep.ts`, International getHelp on every page range: "Your baby is floppy, hard to wake, or breathing fast or with difficulty"
  - `entries/feeding.ts`, International: "Your baby isn't feeding, or hasn't had a wet diaper in 12 hours"
  - `entries/feelings.ts` [0,1]: "Your baby's cry sounds different, weak or high-pitched, or they don't seem like themselves"
  - `entries/feelings.ts` [2,3]: "Your baby cries in bursts and can't be comforted, or isn't acting like themselves"
  - `entries/health.ts` Sweden [0,0] and [1,2]: "Your baby seems very ill, refuses all drinks, or has a dry mouth and wets fewer diapers"
  - `entries/sleep.ts` Sweden: "Your baby seems very ill, or doesn't act like themselves"
- **Rule:** 3, safety.
- **Why:**
  - NHS's under-5 urgent page (the cited `nhs-urgent`) puts every one of these International items under **"Call 999 or go to A&E"**: floppy, hard to wake, weak or high-pitched or continuous cry, breathing difficulty, blue / grey / pale / blotchy skin, non-fading rash, seizure, no pee in 12 h. The NHS colic page also sends a weak or high-pitched cry to A&E / 999.
  - 1177's colic page says **emergency department immediately** for inconsolable crying in bursts, or a baby not acting normally (limp, refuses food, moans).
  - 1177's fever page says contact care **straight away** for a child who seems very ill or refuses all drinks.
  - In the app these lines sit in the same list as "You think your baby may have colic", with only the generic footer ("If you're worried, contact … In an emergency, call …"). Other items in the same lists do say how urgent they are ("Swollen lips…: get emergency help", "A fit with a fever: call 112", "seek care straight away"). These don't, so they read as the lower tier.
- **Fix:** Add the urgency to each item, matching its source:
  - International: "…: call your emergency number now".
  - Sweden: "…: seek care straight away" (1177 fever), or "…: go to the emergency department or call 112" (1177 colic: limp, can't be comforted).
  - Consider a test asserting that every `nhs-urgent` getHelp item ends with an urgency phrase.

### 2. (H) Rotavirus vaccine: the warning about intussusception is missing
- **Where:**
  - `health.ts` `VACCINES[1]` and `VACCINES[3]` (rotavirus at 6 weeks and 3 months), and `VACCINES[5]` (third rotavirus dose).
  - `entries/feelings.ts` [0,1] and [2,3] getHelp, which cover crying in bursts.
- **Rule:** 3, safety (falsely reassuring).
- **Why:**
  - 1177's vaccine page (`1177-vaccines`, cited on these Health pages): very rarely the bowel folds into itself after rotavirus drops; go to an emergency department at once if you suspect it.
  - 1177's colic page: emergency department immediately for crying in bursts that can't be comforted, "especially if rotavirus vaccine in the past week".
  - Instead, the 3-month line says only "Afterwards some babies are tired, eat less or get a fever." A parent reading the page right after the drops gets reassurance and no red flag.
- **Fix:**
  - Add to the 6-week and 3-month lines (and the 5-month line when a 3rd dose is given): "Very rarely, in the week after rotavirus drops, the bowel can fold into itself. Go to the emergency department straight away if your baby cries hard in bursts, turns pale or limp, vomits, or has blood in their poo."
  - Keep the symptom list to what 1177 says, or link the 1177 page.
  - In the Sweden feelings [2,3] item on crying in bursts, add "especially in the week after rotavirus drops: go to the emergency department".

## Medium

### 3. (M) CDC items from a later checklist appear on earlier pages under "about 3 in 4 children do by around this age"
- **Where:** each `now` list below mixes a later CDC checklist into an earlier page. `content.ts` then attaches the CDC framing note to it ("From CDC's checklists: things about 3 in 4 children do by around this age.").
  - 1 month early: `movement.ts` [8,9], `talking.ts` [8,9], `feelings.ts` [8,9] and `play.ts` [8,9] all use `cdc-9-months` on the 8-month page.
  - 2 months early: `feelings.ts` [10,12] uses `cdc-1-year` on the 10-month page.
  - 3 months early:
    - `play.ts` [12,15] uses `cdc-15-months` ("Stack at least two small things", "Try to use things the right way") on the 12-month page.
    - `feelings.ts` [15,18] uses `cdc-18-months` ("Move away from you, but look back…", "Point to show you something interesting") on the 15-month page.
    - `feelings.ts` [21,24] uses `cdc-2-years` on the 21-month page.
  - 6 months early:
    - `play.ts` [24,30] uses `cdc-30-months` ("Use things to pretend, like feeding a block to a doll…", "Play next to other children, and sometimes with them") on the 24-month page.
    - `feelings.ts` [30,36] uses `cdc-3-years` ("Calm down within 10 minutes after you leave…") on the 30-month page.
- **Rule:** 1 (wrong age for the framing) and 5.
- **Why:** CDC's 75% figure is for the checklist age. On the 24-month page, the note claims 3 in 4 children feed a block to a doll "by around this age", but that is the 30-month checklist. That is the kind of comparison that worries parents.
- **Fix:** Either:
  - keep each CDC item only on pages at or after its checklist age (split the entries), or
  - add the checklist age to the item ("by 30 months: …"), and let the framing note say "by the age shown".

### 4. (M) "Doesn't point to show you things" is a get-help sign at 15 months
- **Where:** `entries/feelings.ts` [15,18], getHelp: "Your child doesn't point to show you things" (`cdc-18-months`).
- **Rule:** 3 (alarming about something normal) and 5 (age fit).
- **Why:**
  - Pointing to show is on CDC's **18**-month checklist. At 15 months CDC lists only "points to ask for something or to get help".
  - 1177 6–12 m: some children start pointing at 8 months, "others not until 16 months". The guide says this itself on `talking.ts` [10,11]: "some start at 8 months, others at 16".
  - So on the 15-month page the item flags something that is still within 1177's normal window.
- **Fix:** "By 18 months your child doesn't point to show you things", or move the item to an entry starting at 18.

### 5. (M) "Until 3 months" is shown on the 3-month page
- **Where:** `entries/sleep.ts` [1,3], Sweden tryTogether: "Until 3 months, the safest place to sleep is your baby's own bed in your room."
- **Rule:** 5 (age fit). This is the example named in the brief.
- **Why:**
  - The 3-month page covers 3.0–3.9 months. There the line reads as advice that no longer applies, and nothing replaces it.
  - 1177 doesn't say bed-sharing becomes safe at 3 months. It only says the risk is higher under 3 months and falls as the baby can turn.
- **Fix:**
  - Change it to "Under 3 months, the safest place to sleep is your baby's own bed in your room; the risk falls as they get older and can turn over."
  - Or end this Sweden line at page 2 and give page 3 a line that fits.

### 6. (M) On the 1-month page, the head-lifting items contradict each other
- **Where:** `entries/movement.ts` [1,1].
  - now: "Lift their head when lying on the tummy: somewhere from 1 to 5 months" (`1177-motor-table`).
  - getHelp: "By 6 to 8 weeks your baby doesn't lift their head at all on their tummy" (`rikshandboken-motor-6-8-weeks`).
- **Rule:** 1 and 3 (mixed message, could alarm).
- **Why:** One item says lifting the head can come as late as 5 months. The other sends a parent for help if it hasn't come by 8 weeks. Rikshandboken means a brief lift and turn. 1177's table probably means a steadier lift. The text doesn't make the difference clear.
- **Fix:**
  - Drop the 1177 table item from page 1. The intro and first `now` item already cover the brief lift.
  - Or reword it: "Hold their head up steadily on the tummy: somewhere from 1 to 5 months. A brief lift comes first."

### 7. (M) "Have a high temperature at 38 °C or more" sits in the "Many children around now" list
- **Where:** `entries/health.ts`, International `now` on every page range ([0,0], [1,2], [3,5], [12,18], [21,36]).
- **Rule:** 3 and 4.
- **Why:**
  - Under the title "Many children around now", this reads as a claim that many babies this age have a fever. That is alarming on the Newborn page, where a fever is an emergency.
  - The Sweden version is phrased correctly ("Have a temperature of about 36 to 37.8 °C when well; a fever usually means 38 °C or more").
- **Fix:** Use "Have a normal temperature below 38 °C: 38 °C or more counts as a high temperature" (NHS), or move the line into the intro.

### 8. (M) A fever after the 3- and 5-month vaccines clashes with "fever under 6 months with no cold signs: seek care straight away"
- **Where:**
  - `health.ts`: `AFTER_SHOTS` is "Afterwards some babies are tired, eat less or get a fever." It appears on pages 3 and 5.
  - `entries/health.ts` [3,5], Sweden getHelp: "Your baby is under 6 months, has a fever and no signs of a cold at all: seek care straight away".
- **Rule:** 1 and 3 (mixed message).
- **Why:**
  - Both lines are on the same page. A fever after the shots almost always comes with no cold signs, so a parent can't tell which line applies.
  - 1177 states both, but in separate places.
- **Fix:** Add "If your baby gets a fever after vaccines and you're unsure, call 1177." to the vaccine line or to the getHelp item.

### 9. (M) Parents' corner: thoughts of self-harm get "the same day", and psychosis stops at 1 month
- **Where:** `entries/parents.ts`.
  - "…have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby…: get help the same day" on [0,1], [2,5], [6,11], [12,21] and [24,36].
  - The psychosis item ("seem confused, see or hear things…") exists only on [0,1].
- **Rule:** 3, safety.
- **Why:**
  - 1177's depression page sends postpartum psychosis to a psychiatric emergency or 112. NHS says call 999 or go to A&E if anyone may be in danger.
  - "The same day" is fine for passing thoughts, but too slow if someone may act on them. The parents' contact line does end with "In an emergency, call 112", but the item itself sets the lower bar.
  - 1177 gives psychosis onset from 2 weeks to 3 months after birth, so pages 2–3 also need the psychosis item.
- **Fix:**
  - "…get help today. If you might act on them, or someone may be in danger, call 112 / your local emergency number now."
  - Extend the psychosis item to [2,5], or at least to pages 2–3.

### 10. (M) Sweden's Health pages have no breathing, colour, rash or floppiness red flags
- **Where:** `entries/health.ts`, Sweden getHelp, all ranges.
- **Rule:** 3 (gap) and 2.
- **Why:**
  - International gets concrete signs: breathing difficulty, blue or grey skin, a rash that doesn't fade, floppy, a stiff neck.
  - Sweden gets only fever thresholds and "seems very ill".
  - Nothing in the Swedish sources contradicts those signs. The 1177 fever page just isn't the right source for them.
- **Fix:** Add the same physical red flags to Sweden from a 1177 source (for example its pages on when to seek emergency care for children, or breathing problems), and record that source in `NOTES.md`. If no 1177 source is added, say so in `NOTES.md` as a known gap.

### 11. (M) Separation fear and silence at preschool are flagged with no time frame on the 30-month page
- **Where:** `entries/feelings.ts` [30,36], getHelp:
  - "Your child is still very afraid every time you leave, or worries all the time that something will happen to you"
  - "…or talks at home but not at preschool"
- **Rule:** 3 (alarming about normal) and 1 (overstated).
- **Why:**
  - Rikshandboken says separation anxiety usually fades around 2–3 years, so at 30 months it can still be normal. It is a concern only when it doesn't fade or is excessive for the age.
  - For selective mutism it says "lasting months". Many children are quiet at a new preschool for a while.
- **Fix:**
  - "…very afraid every time you leave, far more than other worries, or still at 3"
  - "…talks at home but has stayed silent at preschool for months"

### 12. (M) "Isn't moving around in any way" is a get-help sign at 8 months
- **Where:** `entries/movement.ts` [8,9], getHelp: "Your baby isn't moving around in any way, or their legs seem floppy or stiff" (`rikshandboken-motor-8-months`).
- **Rule:** 3 and 5.
- **Why:**
  - At 8 months 1177 says only "some" have learned to move forward. WHO's crawling window runs to 13.5 months.
  - Rikshandboken 8 m doesn't make this a referral sign. It lists reasons to look into if a child isn't crawling (no tummy time, floppy or stiff legs). "Doesn't move around" is first a deviation at the **10**-month check.
- **Fix:** Keep the legs part. Change the rest to "By 10 months your baby isn't moving around in any way", or move it to [10,11], which already has "Your baby doesn't move around by themselves".

## Low

### 13. (L) Coming-up text promises a language check at the 2.5-year visit
- **Where:** `entries/pages.ts` page 24, comingUp Sweden: "…and the BVC visit at 2.5 years with a check of language."
- **Rule:** 1 (overstated).
- **Why:** The language screening is at one of the 2.5 **or** 3 year visits (Rikshandboken PDF, `NOTES.md`). `health.ts` gets this right ("at this visit or the next").
- **Fix:** "…and the BVC visit at 2.5 years, where language may be checked."

### 14. (L) "Walk heel to toe" at 30 months
- **Where:** `entries/movement.ts` [30,30], intro: "many children walk heel to toe".
- **Rule:** 1.
- **Why:** Rikshandboken means a relaxed heel-to-toe gait (no longer wide-based). In everyday English "walk heel to toe" means tandem walking along a line, a much later skill. A parent could test that and worry.
- **Fix:** "many children walk with a relaxed, grown-up step, no longer wide-legged".

### 15. (L) Parents' corner shows "a few days after the birth" on the 1-month page
- **Where:** `entries/parents.ts` [0,1].
  - now: "Feel weepy, low or up and down a few days after the birth"
  - intro: "…usually pass within 2 weeks"
- **Rule:** 5.
- **Why:** On the 1-month page (4–8 weeks) a "now" item about the first days is out of date. By then, low mood falls under the getHelp item instead.
- **Fix:** Split [0,0] and [1,1], or reword: "Have felt weepy or up and down in the first weeks; for most it has eased by now".

### 16. (L) Rolling and sitting at 6 months: stricter than Sweden's own sources
- **Where:** `entries/movement.ts` [6,7].
  - getHelp: "Your baby makes no attempt to roll, even at home" (on the 6-month page).
  - now: "Sit without support: healthy children get there anywhere from about 4 to 9 months" (`who-motor`).
- **Rule:** 2 and 1.
- **Why:**
  - Rolling: 1177's table puts rolling at 3–8 months. Rikshandboken's response to no attempts at 6 m is to encourage floor play and tummy time, not a referral.
  - Sitting: 1177's own window is 5–10 months, so a Swedish parent of a 9.5-month-old is told they're outside "healthy" when 1177 says they aren't. Also, "anywhere from" hides that WHO's window covers the 1st to 99th percentile.
- **Fix:**
  - Rolling: "By about 8 months your baby makes no attempt to roll", or move the item to [7,7] and later.
  - Sitting: "nearly all healthy children get there between about 4 and 9 to 10 months", citing both WHO and `1177-motor-table`.

### 17. (L) Newborn feeding: "needs waking" and "hard to wake" pull in opposite directions
- **Where:** `entries/feeding.ts` [0,0], Sweden.
  - tryTogether: "A very sleepy newborn may need waking for feeds."
  - getHelp: "…or your baby seems hard to wake for feeds or unsettled after them"
- **Rule:** 3 (mixed message, and under-triage).
- **Why:**
  - The page calls a sleepy newborn normal and then lists "hard to wake" as a concern, with no line between the two.
  - NHS treats "hard to wake" as 999. Mild sleepiness is a feeding question for BVC.
- **Fix:** "…seems unusually hard to wake, or too sleepy to feed: call 1177 or seek care straight away". Keep "may need waking" in tryTogether.

### 18. (L) "Stop vitamin D drops at 2" is a "now" item on the 21-month page
- **Where:** `entries/health.ts` [21,36], Sweden now: "Stop vitamin D drops at 2, though some need them for longer".
- **Rule:** 5.
- **Why:** On the 21-month page a "Many children around now: Stop vitamin D drops" item can read as "stop now". Drops still continue until 2 there, and the personal Vitamin D line says so.
- **Fix:** "Keep D-drops every day until 2; some need them for longer".

### 19. (L) The 1177 point about arguing is stretched past what the source says
- **Where:** `entries/parents.ts` [6,11], now: "Argue more when they're worn out, which doesn't mean the relationship is in trouble" (`1177-crying`).
- **Rule:** 1 (overstated).
- **Why:** 1177 says that arguing **about the crying** doesn't mean the relationship is in trouble. The app makes it a general claim about all arguing.
- **Fix:** "Argue more when they're worn out, for example over a crying baby. That doesn't mean the relationship is in trouble."

### 20. (L) British and American words are mixed
- **Where:**
  - "dummy" in `talking.ts` [6,7] and [10,11]
  - "cot" in `sleep.ts` International (several items) and `feelings.ts` [0,1]
  - "trousers" in `movement.ts` [30,30] and [36,36]
  - against "diaper" and "stroller" elsewhere
- **Rule:** 4 (the app uses "diaper", not "nappy").
- **Why:** The rule names only one word, but its intent is one consistent register.
- **Fix:** Use "pacifier", "crib" and "pants", or decide that only "diaper" is fixed and note that in `NOTES.md`.

### 21. (L) Some NHS sentences are copied too closely
- **Where:**
  - `parents.ts` [0,1]: "Sleep when your baby sleeps: rest matters more than housework." NHS: rest when the baby sleeps; rest matters more than housework.
  - `play.ts` [4,5]: "…even 10 minutes a day helps." NHS: "even 10 minutes a day helps".
  - `talking.ts` [15,15]: "Yes, it is a cat." and "Apple or banana?" (NHS examples).
  - `talking.ts` [18,21]: "Get your coat" (NHS example).
- **Rule:** 6 (own words for NHS, per `MAIN.md`, even though OGL would allow reuse with attribution).
- **Fix:** Rephrase, for example:
  - "Nap when your baby naps; the housework can wait."
  - "a few minutes of books every day helps"
  - "Yes, a dog!"
  - "Milk or water?"
  - "Find your shoes"

### 22. (L) International Newborn Health states the UK schedule as a general fact
- **Where:** `entries/health.ts` [0,0], International now: "Have a physical check within 72 hours of birth, of the eyes, heart and hips among other things, and a hearing test" (`nhs-reviews`).
- **Rule:** 2.
- **Why:** The 72-hour timing and the newborn hearing test are NHS programme facts, not international ones. `checkUps()` says it the right way: "usually … in the first days … many countries".
- **Fix:** "Have a physical check in the first days after birth, and in many countries a hearing test."

### 23. (L) Advice on baby walkers disagrees between pages
- **Where:** `entries/movement.ts`.
  - [4,5] tryTogether: "Keep time in bouncers, walkers and baby seats short: no more than 20 minutes at a time."
  - [10,11] and [12,12]: "Skip baby walkers."
- **Rule:** 1 (mixed message).
- **Why:** The NHS allows a limit. CDC says avoid. A parent reading both pages gets two answers.
- **Fix:** Pick one. For example, drop "walkers" from the 4–5 line ("bouncers and baby seats"), since a 4-month-old isn't in a walker anyway.

### 24. (L) The typical sleep line presents AASM / NSF recommendations as a description
- **Where:** `sleep.ts` `typicalSleep`: "Many children this age sleep {min}–{max} hours in 24 hours, naps included."
- **Rule:** 1.
- **Why:**
  - AASM and NSF give recommended amounts, not what many children sleep.
  - AASM gives no range at all under 4 months because normal variation is wide. The NSF's 14–17 h stands in there.
  - Low risk, since the comparison shows no verdict.
- **Fix:**
  - International: "Sleep experts suggest {min}–{max} hours in 24 hours at this age, naps included."
  - Under 4 months, add "Sleep varies a lot at this age."
